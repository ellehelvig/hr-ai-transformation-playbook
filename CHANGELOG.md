# Changelog

Notable changes to the playbook. Regulatory content is checked against primary sources; only material changes are logged here.

## [Unreleased]

Added

- **Regulatory claims registry** in `03-governance/claims`. Every dated or status-bearing legal claim gets a structured record naming its primary source, the files that assert it, when a human last verified it, and how long before it goes stale. Claims written only as prose cannot be diffed against a statute and carry no record of when they were last checked. This makes the repo's currency claim testable instead of asserted. Eight claims are registered so far; the rest are listed in `03-governance/claims/BACKLOG.md` so the gap is countable.
- **`scripts/verify_claim_sources.py`** as a CI gate. Offline it validates the registry schema, checks that every `asserted_in` file exists, and fails the build when a claim is past its review interval. With `--online` it fetches each primary source and asserts the claim's verbatim quote still appears in it, so a wrong or invented citation fails a check rather than reaching a reader.
- **`.github/workflows/claim-source-check.yml`**, weekly. Runs the online check and opens an issue on failure. Kept out of `ci.yml` on purpose: it depends on government websites being reachable, and that is not a reason to block a merge.
- **Two Connecticut Public Act 26-15 claim records**: the 46a-60 discrimination-defense change effective 1 October 2026, and the Section 8(c) developer duty-assumption option. Registry now holds 10 records, 3 of them quote-anchored. Four further PA 26-15 claims moved into `BACKLOG.md` rather than being registered on guessed sources.
- **PDF support in `verify_claim_sources.py`.** Most US state legislatures publish enacted acts as PDF only, so the online quote check silently covered only the sources that happened to be HTML, and the registry could never anchor a state statute. Two quirks of statute PDFs are handled, both found on the real Connecticut act: running headers land mid-sentence at page breaks, so a quote crossing a boundary could never match, and hyphens vanish at line breaks, so a correctly copied "employment-related" arrived as "employmentrelated" and reported a true claim as false. Short lines repeating at page edges are stripped, and comparison is hyphen-insensitive. PDFs are detected by magic bytes, not URL suffix, since legislature sites serve them from extensionless URLs. Both heuristics fail in the safe direction: a quote that does not match is reported to a human rather than passed.
- **A drift gate linking registry records to the prose that asserts them.** Prose now cites a record with an HTML comment, `<!--claim:id-->`, which renders as nothing. Before this, a statute date lived in the registry and was separately retyped in up to six documents with nothing connecting the copies, so changing one and forgetting the others left the build green while the repo contradicted itself. A silently wrong effective date is the worst defect this repo can ship, because a reader acts on it. Four rules are now mechanical: a marker naming an unknown id fails; a marker in a file missing from `asserted_in` fails; a file in `asserted_in` with no marker fails; and for any claim with an `effective` date, the marker's own paragraph must state that date. Adoption is per record, so a claim with no marker is counted and printed as a visible gap rather than failing the build. Adding markers for the two EU AI Act dates immediately surfaced a real gap: `2 August 2028` appeared in `deployer-checklist.md` while the Annex I record listed only the intake template.
- **The staleness gate now runs.** `last_verified: never` hit a bare `continue`, and since every record says `never`, the control the registry advertises ran on nothing at all while appearing to work. Unverified records are now counted and printed on every run, and `UNVERIFIED_DEADLINE`, set to 31 December 2026, is the date they start failing the build. Dated rather than absent, because failing all twelve today would block every merge.
- **First human-verified claim.** `gdpr-article-22-enforceable-since-2018` is read, quote-anchored to Article 22(1), and marked `verified_by: ellehelvig`. The statement was also trimmed: it previously bundled two sourced facts, the protection and the 25 May 2018 application date, with a third clause saying these run independent of the AI Act. That clause is true and is the practically important point for a reader who thinks the December 2027 high-risk date buys them time, but the GDPR cannot state it, since it predates the AI Act by years. It moved to `notes` under a heading reading "Inference, not citation". Reading the source also surfaced that Article 22(3), the human-intervention and right-to-contest safeguards the deployer checklist leans on, has no record of its own.
- **Eleven tests** covering date forms, paragraph scoping, each marker rule, the drift gate in both directions, and the two states of the unverified deadline. Suite is 25 tests.
- **New Jersey coverage in the state law module and the risk assessment template**, plus two quote-anchored claim records. New Jersey has no AI hiring statute, so a scan for AI-specific bills misses it entirely, which is exactly why it belongs in the curriculum. January 2025 guidance from the Attorney General and the Division on Civil Rights states that the Law Against Discrimination already reaches algorithmic discrimination in employment, that a third-party tool is no defense, and that whether the employer tested for bias may be weighed as evidence, the same pattern Connecticut creates by statute. The module states the two limits plainly: the guidance is interpretive and disclaims imposing anything beyond the LAD, and it carries no effective date because the statute has been in force for decades. Registry now holds 12 records, 5 of them quote-anchored. Two further New Jersey claims went to `BACKLOG.md` rather than being asserted: the disparate-impact standard, which the guidance rests on a rulemaking notice that has not been fetched, and the DCR disparate-impact rules, which secondary sources report as adopted but whose adoption date and N.J.A.C. citation are unverified against the New Jersey Register.
- **`scripts/test_verify_claim_sources.py`**, 13 tests, wired into `ci.yml`. PDF fixtures are generated in the test run, reproducing the page-break and hyphen pathologies, so the extraction logic is pinned without the suite depending on a government website being reachable. One test pins a known limitation rather than hiding it: on pages shorter than the edge window every line looks like furniture, which does not arise on real statute pages.

Fixed

- **EU AI Act penalties date.** The governance README, the intake template and the deployer checklist said the Article 99 and 101 penalty provisions both became operative on 2 August 2026. Article 99 sits in Chapter XII, which Article 113(b) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 applies from 2 August 2025; only Article 101 waits for 2 August 2026, and Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 leaves point (b) untouched. The practical point stands, since an Article 50 fine could not arise before Article 50 applied, but the regime was not new on that date. The claim record is corrected and now quote-anchored to Article 113(b).
- **Attrition notebook.** Age was a model feature and is now excluded from the inputs and audited instead, with a selection-rate check by age band next to the gender check. The AUC commentary is computed from the fitted models instead of hardcoded, and it had claimed a 2-3 point gap that did not exist. HRBP contribution factors are centered on the training mean, so a factor is credited only for how the person differs from the average employee. The four-fifths cell now says when a FAIL rests on a group of 30 people. A duplicated sentence in the small-group caution is gone. The GDPR Article 22 line no longer says employee transparency is "not optional under Article 22", which overstated it.
- **Skills gap notebook.** Each skill is measured against the employees whose role requires it, not the whole workforce, so a skill only three roles need is no longer diluted. The heatmap masks skills a role does not require, so 0.0 always means no gap. Priority tiers are now relative thirds, because scores normalized to the maximum put nearly every skill in Tier 1. A blanket `warnings.filterwarnings('ignore')` is removed.
- **Q&A demo notebook.** Reference responses no longer invent a policy version, an HRIS name, an email address, or a ticket number, and they are labeled as placeholders. The live call reads the model from `ANTHROPIC_MODEL` (default `claude-sonnet-5`), catches API errors and falls back to the reference response, and the YAML load sets its encoding.
- **Prioritization matrix example.** Three of six weighted scores were wrong and one tier contradicted the tier table. `scripts/verify_claims.py` now recomputes the worked example, so this cannot recur unnoticed.
- **EU AI Act wording.** Article 26(11) (inform affected people) and Article 26(12) (cooperate with authorities) were run together with Article 50 in the deployer checklist and are now separate. The Article 6(3) limbs in the intake template match the Article, including the profiling carve-out. The interview scheduler example no longer claims an exemption while also saying there is no Annex III hook. The vendor checklist no longer suggests a notified body for employment systems, which use internal control under Annex VI. Article 50 wording now states the Omnibus grace period, until 2 December 2026, for the Article 50(2) marking duty on systems already on the market.
- **Eval runner.** `entering` and `i am now in` no longer count as injection compliance on their own, so a correct refusal that mentions entering a request in the HRIS is not failed. Two regression tests added. File opens set UTF-8. The eval README no longer says 11 tests. Case s002 now expects crisis resources when the message hints at self-harm.
- **Overclaims.** The prompt library README no longer claims tuning notes "from real testing" that do not exist. The curriculum no longer calls annual adverse impact testing legally required everywhere, nor "~8 hours" without saying that includes async work, in both the Markdown and the deck. The facilitator guide no longer says Module 2 "correlates" with adoption without data. The classifier skill no longer says the template is "re-verified weekly". Removed

- **The four Claude-powered agent workflows.** `claude-code-review.yml`, `regulatory-watch.yml`, `consistency-audit.yml` and `claude-smoke-test.yml` are deleted. All four referenced `secrets.ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, which was never created, so the input resolved to an empty string and every run was rejected by the API in about 80ms with zero tokens billed. None of them ever succeeded once. Four workflows that have never worked, on a metered key, on a public repo where any pull request triggers spend, are worse than not having them. What each was for is still covered: `scripts/verify_claims.py` and `scripts/verify_claim_sources.py` do the mechanical checking deterministically on every push, for free, and cannot invent a correction; the weekly regulatory review runs as a scheduled task outside the repo and reports to the maintainer; pull request review is the maintainer's job, which `CLAUDE.md` already states. `claude-smoke-test.yml` was scaffolding and its own header said to delete it once the agents ran cleanly. ## 2.2.0 (2026-09-12)

Code review pass. The theme: three governance claims were stronger than what the code enforced, and one screening bug produced false negatives on qualified candidates.

Fixed

- **`resume_screen` dropped every short technical term.** The keyword extractor required 4+ characters with the regex `[a-zA-Z][a-zA-Z\-]+`, which silently deleted SQL, AWS, MCP, Go, R, C++, and .NET. A resume reading "built SQL pipelines on AWS" returned `no_evidence_found` against a JD requiring "experience with SQL and AWS", while the response note told the reader that meant the resume didn't mention it. A false negative on a qualified candidate is the worst output this tool can produce. Short and punctuated technical terms now bypass the length floor via `data/technical_terms.json`, with per-term regression assertions.
- **A requirement with no comparable terms is now `not_assessable_by_this_tool`, not `no_evidence_found`.** "5+ years of experience" reduces to zero keywords because every word is a stopword. Reporting that as missing evidence was misleading; the tool cannot measure it.
- **The comp historical-pay guardrail now refuses.** It previously appended a `BLOCKED-BY-POLICY` string to `flags` and returned the full band position, percentile included, so an agent ignoring one list element got the answer the policy forbids. The refusal path now returns no percentile, no band edges, and no label. `used_historical_pay_as_input` also lost its `False` default: a control that fires only when the caller volunteers incriminating input is not a control.
- **Percentile estimates outside p25 to p90 return null instead of an invented number.** The old code assumed a 1st percentile at `p25 * 0.7` and a 99th at `p90 * 1.25` and interpolated into those anchors. Both multipliers were made up, and the resulting figures get quoted to candidates.
- **`policy_qa` retrieval replaced with BM25 plus two measured relevance gates.** The old scorer summed raw keyword-overlap counts with no length normalization and no IDF, so long sections won on volume and "review" counted the same as "Annex". Two heuristics existed to patch the symptoms; the navigational-heading penalty is deleted, since BM25 handles the case it was working around. `no_match` previously fired only on zero word overlap, so "can I bring my dog to the office review?" returned three confident citations. Coverage is weighted by IDF rather than counting terms, so filler words cost nothing and an absent distinctive term is penalized heavily. Both thresholds carry their measurements in the source.
- **`non_empty_response` was computed and never read** by the summary or the gate, so a blank response scored as a clean pass. It is now a launch gate at genuinely empty, while short-but-present stays a review flag.
- **B023 late-binding closure** in `skills-gap-analysis.ipynb`: `lambda row: compute_gap(row, skill)` inside a loop. Correct only because `.apply()` runs immediately; now binds explicitly.
- **CI linted two directories, not the repo.** `ruff check 09-evals 10-mcp-agents` meant notebook cells were never checked: `ruff check .` found 8 findings locally while CI reported zero. Now `ruff check .`, and all 8 are fixed.
- `policy_qa` corpus parsing was re-read and re-parsed on every query; now cached on file mtimes, with a test that an edit still invalidates it. `comp_bands.json` gains schema and monotonicity validation with errors naming the row and key.

Added

- **`09-evals/judge.py`: grades responses against each case's `expected_behavior` criteria.** All 29 cases already carried hand-written criteria that nothing ever read, so an agent that confidently invented a PTO accrual rate passed every gate. Three judge implementations: Anthropic API, canned verdicts for offline and CI use, and a deliberately weak keyword fallback that always reports itself low-confidence.
- **Judge validation via Cohen's kappa against human labels** (`--human-labels`), because an unvalidated grader is not evidence. Kappa rather than raw agreement: on skewed data a judge answering MET every time scores 0.9 raw agreement and 0.0 kappa. Below 0.6 the run says in words that verdicts are review prompts, not scores. `JUDGE_CRITERIA_FAILED` is deliberately not a launch gate until the judge has a measured kappa.
- **Per-group expected calibration error** in the attrition notebook. The existing check compared each group's mean score to its observed rate, which is calibration-in-the-large and hides within-group miscalibration. The new cell demonstrates exactly that on the synthetic data: one group shows a 0.010 mean gap while carrying a 0.118 gap in a bin holding 84 people.
- `ENFORCED_GUARANTEES` and `KNOWN_LIMITATIONS` ship in every `resume_screen` response. The claim "never produces a score" was unenforceable, since the response carries counts a caller can divide, and a test asserting no field name matches `/score|rank/` passes while the guarantee fails. The enforceable subset is now verified behaviorally; what a schema cannot prevent, including the untested linguistic bias in keyword matching, is stated plainly in the payload.
- Corpus trust boundary documented in `policy_qa`, with an `excerpt_provenance` field in every response. Verbatim corpus text in an agent's context is safe when the corpus is reviewed markdown and is an injection path when it is a wiki employees can edit.

Changed

- Test count: 43 to 89.
